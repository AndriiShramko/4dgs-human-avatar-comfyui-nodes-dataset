## Executive Summary

**Flex4DHuman** (arXiv 2606.13655, released 2026-06-11) by University of Washington + **World Labs** (Fei-Fei Li's company, $1B raised) generates synchronized **dense multi-view videos** of a human actor from a **monocular or sparse-view video**, then reconstructs dynamic Gaussian splats with off-the-shelf 4DGS optimization. It directly attacks **our Risk #1** (occluded-region hallucination, Section 6.2) by placing a web-scale generative video prior *upstream* of reconstruction instead of our planned *downstream* 1-step refinement.

**Verdict**: replicate the **architecture** (publicly described, legally free to re-implement via our Clean-Room process), do **not** touch their data or future weights (training datasets are non-commercial), and ship it as an offline **Quality Mode** alongside the v6 feed-forward **Fast Mode** (<3s, deferred to v2). This converts our hardest research risk into a fine-tuning task on a 1.3B Apache-2.0 backbone.

## 8.1 What They Built

Pipeline **[A]** (quoted from project page): *"Given a monocular or sparse-view actor video, it first generates synchronized dense multi-view videos. We then segment the foreground actor and fit the generated views to reconstruct dynamic Gaussian splats."*

| Component | Their solution | Confidence |
|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Backbone | Wan 2.1 **1.3B** T2V DiT; **only** architectural change = positional encoding in self-attention | [A-pdf] |
| Conditioning | Relative camera pose only, via **PRoPE** (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025 "Cameras as Relative Positional Encoding") on a dedicated attention band | [A-pdf] |
| Geometry priors | **None** — no SMPL, skeleton, depth, or normals, at train or inference | [A-pdf] |
| Training curriculum | 3 stages (details in §8.1.1); 32×H100; 30k+30k+30k+15k iterations | [A-pdf] |
| Long video | Chunked rollout, overlap O frames reused as clean history tokens; teacher-forced history training | [A-pdf] |
| Input flexibility | N_ref ∈ {1...15} at train; 1/2/4 refs evaluated: 25.21 → 28.62 → 31.90 dB PSNR (monotonic) | [A-pdf] |
| Text control | Multi-view captions by **Gemini 3 Flash** (25,031 captions, avg 268 words, appearance-focused — motion captions were unreliable); CFG dropout 0.1, inference CFG 3.0 | [A-pdf] |
| Segmentation | **MatAnyone2** — the same tool already shipped in our ComfyUI-CustomNodePacks | [A-pdf] |
| 4DGS stage | **FreeTimeGS** (CVPR 2025, zju3dv) fitting on generated views | [A-pdf] |
| Training data | **DNA-Rendering only** (Diffuman4D processed release: 1,038 seqs / 548 IDs / 48 cams); **ActorsHQ is zero-shot eval only**, NOT training; DFA (Artemis) for a separate animal fine-tune | [A-pdf] — corrects our earlier assumption that ActorsHQ was in training |

Repo: github.com/Andy-Cheng/Flex4DHuman. **Reproducibility after full PDF review: ~7.5/10** — architecture, curriculum, band allocation, rollout protocol, and eval protocol are fully specified; still missing: optimizer/LR/batch size, default rollout overlap O in production, and view-sampling details (minor — standard values recoverable by ablation).

### 8.1.1 Technical specification extracted from the paper (Clean-Room spec input)

- **Token layout**: views packed view-major; per-view input = **36 channels** = 16 noisy latents + 16 conditioning latents (encoded reference latents for refs, zeros for targets) + 4-channel binary mask (inherited from Wan 2.1 I2V). Input projection expanded 16→36ch by copying pretrained weights and zero-initializing new ones. Conv3D(1×2×2) spatial patchifier.
- **Positional encoding**: Wan's per-head RoPE allocation (D_t, D_h, D_w) = (44, 42, 42) for head dim 128 is repartitioned to **(D_time, D_view, D_SE(3), D_h, D_w) = (16, 8, 20, 42, 42)**. The SE(3) band applies PRoPE: Q_i ← T_i^⊤ Q_i, K_j ← T_j^{-1} K_j (camera poses as relative transforms inside attention; no extra channels or learnable camera params). Camera normalization: all poses expressed relative to the first camera, translations scaled to unit distance.
- **Objective**: Wan's flow matching (velocity prediction), logit-normal timestep schedule, uniform token loss; text dropped to zero-embedding with p=0.1 for CFG.
- **Curriculum**: Stage 1 — 1 ref / 1 target, T=1, 256², 30k iters (adapts backbone to new PE). Stage 2.1 — V=16 total views, N_ref ∈ {1..15}, T=1, 256², random background-drop augmentation, 30k. Stage 2.2 — same at 512², 30k. Stage 3 — dynamic V×T at 512² with teacher-forced history; multitasked layouts {32×1, 16×2, 8×4} and {8×8, 4×16, 2×32}, 15k iters. Hardware: 32×H100.
- **Inference**: 40 denoising steps, CFG 3.0. Rollout: denoise T-frame chunk across all views; advance T−O frames; reuse O overlapping predictions as clean history. T=4 vs T=16 at O=1 give similar quality (24.79 vs 24.86 dB) — short chunks are the memory-efficient operating point.
- **Headline metrics** (foreground-only): DNA-Rendering 16-scene test, 1 ref / 47 targets: **25.44 PSNR / 0.9516 SSIM / 0.0617 LPIPS** vs Diffuman4D-GT-skeleton 24.23 (which uses oracle multi-view skeletons) and vs monocular baselines Diffuman4D-mono 16.12 / MV-Performer 17.44. Reference-azimuth robustness: <1 dB variation. Zero-shot ActorsHQ (FreeTimeGS re-render): 21.32 vs 17.97. Animals (DFA fine-tune): 22.16 within-species / 20.32 cross-species.
- **Their stated limitations** (= our opportunities): training dominated by static studio rigs → weak on dynamic cameras, in-the-wild scenes, extreme viewpoints (top-down/tilt); long-horizon rollout drift (they point to Self-Forcing / diffusion forcing as future work — already in our §6.9 plan).

## 8.3 Impact on the v6 Architecture

What Flex4DHuman changes:

1. **Risk #1 mitigation upgraded.** Section 6.2's two-stage hybrid (voxel-LRM trained from scratch + 1-step refinement) carried the highest risk in the plan. Generating dense views *before* reconstruction reduces the reconstruction problem from sparse-view hallucination to dense-view fitting — an order of magnitude easier. Note: the fact-check audit found the original "5-15 dB occluded gap" figure was unsourced (documented gap ~6 dB), but the qualitative risk stands — HumanSplat itself needs a ~9s diffusion stage for unseen regions.
2. **Two product modes instead of one.** Multi-view video diffusion cannot meet the <3s budget (it runs tens of seconds to minutes). Therefore:
   - **Quality Mode (MVP, new)**: monocular/sparse video → preprocessing (matting/stabilization via existing ComfyUI-CustomNodePacks nodes) → multi-view generation (our Wan 2.1 fine-tune) → 4DGS fitting → compression (6.6) → ComfyUI nodes. Offline, minutes per clip.
   - **Fast Mode (v2, unchanged)**: the v6 feed-forward voxel-LRM + refinement path, <3s. Now trains on a *dense-view* task, with Quality-Mode outputs usable as additional supervision.
3. **MVP no longer requires training the LRM at all.** Flex4DHuman itself uses off-the-shelf per-clip 4DGS optimization. Quality-Mode MVP = Wan fine-tune + existing gsplat optimization + our compression + nodes. The Clean-Room transformer (Modules 1 in 6.7) moves to v2.
4. **Body model demoted from survival mechanism to feature.** Their geometry-prior-free result shows MHR/Anny is not required for hole-filling; it remains valuable for re-posing/animation (Section 6.3 tiers) — and sidesteps the loose-clothing topology risk.
5. **Our differentiators remain untouched**: feed-forward speed (v2), compression (they ship raw splats), temporal anti-flicker regularizers (6.3 — diffusion outputs will still have cross-view inconsistencies), ComfyUI distribution, and clean training data.
6. **New gap identified**: moving-camera input. Flex4DHuman assumes static-camera monocular video; arbitrary user video needs a camera pose estimator (VGGT / MegaSaM class) in preprocessing — added to the roadmap below.

Revised economics: training becomes a 1.3B fine-tune instead of from-scratch pretraining — roughly **$10-20K** compute for the generation model (vs $57K plan), with the LRM budget deferred to v2. Quality-Mode MVP timeline: **~4-6 months** (vs 9-13).

- Curriculum stage 1 (pose following) on synthetic + small-rig data; 2-4× H100.

- End-to-end prototype on 1 subject: monocular → matting → multi-view gen → 4DGS → 360° render.

**Phase 2 (months 3-6)**:

**Data strategy (critical path)** — the proprietary rig is not yet operational; until then: (a) synthetic renders from commercially licensed 3D scans (verify ML-training rights per vendor), (b) small synchronized multi-camera captures (4-8 views suffice for curriculum stage 1), (c) partner capture data via LOI clients.

## 8.5 Red-Team Constraints (do not skip)

1. **Never ship or fine-tune their weights** when released — presumed contaminated by NC datasets.
2. **Do not market "100% commercially legal" or "absolute temporal stability"** until the IP review clears the full chain (incl. Wan provenance) and flicker is measured on real user videos. Diffusion → 4DGS composition cannot guarantee *absolute* stability by construction.
3. **360° claims need a disclaimer**: unseen regions (back side) are model hallucinations — faces/tattoos/logos on the back will be invented.
4. Cheap validation before heavy spend: demand test via a research demo (Diffuman4D permits research use) + waitlist; ~$500 flicker/size measurement on 5 dirty user videos.
