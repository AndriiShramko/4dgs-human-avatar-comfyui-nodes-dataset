## Executive Summary

Five verified findings materially improve the v6/6.8 plan:

1. **The fine-tune is cheaper than budgeted.** Camera-conditioned fine-tunes of exactly our backbone (Wan 2.1 1.3B) already exist in the open: **ReCamMaster** (MIT, KwaiVGI) released a Wan2.1-1.3B camera-control checkpoint trained on 8 GPUs at batch size 1 per GPU, on **synthetic UE5 multi-camera data** — and community LoRAs of Wan 1.3B train on a single 24 GB consumer GPU in hours. The $10-20K fine-tune estimate in 6.8 can be staged: LoRA-first validation (~$1-3K), full fine-tune only if conditioning doesn't take hold (~$7-13K).
2. **Quality-Mode inference can drop from minutes to seconds.** **FastWan** (Hao AI Lab, UCSD) distills Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B from 50 → **3 denoising steps** via DMD2 + Video Sparse Attention; a 5-second 480p video takes **5 s end-to-end on one H200 (1 s denoising) and 21 s on one RTX 4090** — with weights, recipe, and dataset under **Apache 2.0**. **Self-Forcing** (same backbone) distills in **<16 h on 8× H100** (≈ **$300-500** at 2026 spot prices) with code Apache-2.0. Distilling *our* fine-tune is a cheap add-on, not a new project. Training-free **TeaCache** (~2x, LoRA-compatible) is the zero-effort fallback.
3. **Per-clip 4DGS fitting — the second bottleneck — has a verified 10-30x path.** **Hybrid 3D-4D Gaussian Splatting** fits a 10-second dynamic clip in **12 minutes vs 5.5 hours** for baseline 4DGS (N3V dataset, 32.25 dB PSNR, 208 FPS render); **Instant4D** reconstructs uncalibrated monocular video in ~5 minutes with 92% fewer Gaussians and also solves our moving-camera-input gap (MegaSaM + UniDepth front-end). Combined with Taming-3DGS-style budgeted densification (>5x time/size reduction) and gsplat's MCMC strategy, per-clip cost falls to **~$0.50-1 [estimate]**.
4. **Backbone check: stay on Wan, evaluate Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B.** Wan 2.2 (released 2025-07-28) is **Apache 2.0**; the TI2V-5B dense model with the 64x-compression Wan2.2-VAE runs 720p/24fps on a single RTX 4090, and a FastWan2.2-TI2V-5B distill already exists. **HunyuanVideo fails our license constraint** (Tencent Community License: 100M-MAU cap + territorial restrictions); **LTX-Video weights are OpenRAIL-M** (commercial but use-restricted — not Apache/MIT/BSD).
5. **Compute is cheaper than the 6.5 tables assume at the margin.** 2026 H100 market median is $2.29-3.12/hr; RunPod $1.99-2.69, Vast.ai marketplace ~$1.49-1.87 (spot interruptible to ~$0.34); consumer 24 GB cards verifiably suffice for all LoRA-scale experiments (musubi-tuner FP8 + block-swap), moving most dev spend off H100s.

**Net effect on Track A**: MVP compute budget **~$121K → ~$62-70K [estimate]**, Quality-Mode per-avatar latency **minutes → roughly 1-3 minutes total (dominated by 4DGS fitting, not diffusion) [estimate]**, timeline unchanged at 4-6 months with distillation added as a 2-3 week parallel task.

## 9.1 Cheaper / Faster Fine-Tuning of the Multi-View Video Model

| Finding | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Wan 2.1 1.3B LoRA trains on one 24 GB consumer GPU | RTX 3090: 3500 steps ≈ 2.5 h (anime-style LoRA); small face LoRA ≈ 20-30 min on a 4070 Ti Super | [B] community tutorials/HF pages (refs 1-3) |
| FP8-base + block-swap training fits Wan in 24 GB | kohya-ss **musubi-tuner**: `--fp8_base --fp8_scaled --blocks_to_swap` enables 720p-image training and 81-frame video inference in 24 GB | [A] repo docs (ref 4) |
| Precompute text/VAE embeddings before training | DiffSynth-Studio moves all non-gradient computation (text encoding, VAE) to a preprocessing stage — "faster speed, less VRAM" | [A] repo (ref 5) |
| Camera-conditioned full fine-tune of Wan2.1-1.3B is modest-compute | **ReCamMaster** (MIT): released checkpoint built on Wan2.1-1.3B; trained on 8 GPUs, batch size 1/GPU; data = **MultiCamVideo**, 13.6K UE5 scenes × 10 cameras = 136K videos | [A] repo (ref 6) |
| Multi-view conditioning works as a plug-in module on a frozen DiT | **SynCamMaster** (ICLR'25): multi-view synchronization module inserted per DiT block of a pre-trained T2V model; camera extrinsics via a small camera encoder | [A] paper/repo (ref 7) |
| Single-GPU Wan 2.2 fine-tuning is documented | AMD ROCm blog: "Fine-tuning Wan 2.2 on a single GPU" | [B] (ref 8) |

**Recommendations:**
- **Stage the fine-tune**: (a) LoRA + SynCamMaster-style sync-module on frozen 1.3B backbone, trained on synthetic UE5-style data — kill-criterion test for ~$1-3K [estimate]; (b) full 3-stage Flex4DHuman curriculum only if (a) plateaus.
- **Copy the ReCamMaster data trick**: UE5-rendered synchronized multi-camera clips are proven sufficient to teach Wan-1.3B camera control. This de-risks the 6.8 data-strategy gap (rig not yet operational) with a fully ownable synthetic corpus. (Their MultiCamVideo dataset itself: check license before any use; rendering our own UE5 data avoids the question entirely.)
- Keep FP8 Transformer Engine for the full fine-tune (per 6.5); for LoRA runs, musubi-tuner/DiffSynth-class tooling on 4090s is sufficient and ~5-8x cheaper per GPU-hour [estimate].

## 9.2 Faster Quality-Mode Inference (Diffusion Stage)

| Method | What it does | Verified numbers | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FastWan** (FastVideo, Hao AI Lab UCSD) | "Sparse distillation": DMD2 + trainable Video Sparse Attention; 50 → 3 steps | FastWan2.1-T2V-1.3B: 5-s 480p video in **5 s on H200 (1 s denoising), 21 s on RTX 4090 (2.8 s denoising)**; VSA cuts attention ~60% | **Apache 2.0** (weights + recipe + 600K-sample synthetic dataset) (refs 9-10) |
| **Self-Forcing** | Autoregressive few-step distillation of Wan2.1-1.3B fixing CausVid's train-test mismatch; data-free (DMD variant) | Real-time streaming on a single RTX 4090; training: 600 iters in <2 h on 64× H100, or **<16 h on 8× H100** with grad accumulation | Code Apache-2.0; weights license unspecified — **we retrain anyway** (ref 11) |
| **CausVid** (CVPR 2025) | Bidirectional teacher → causal few-step student; ODE-trajectory init | 9.4 FPS streaming on a single GPU; VBench-Long 84.27 | (ref 12) |
| **TeaCache** (CVPR 2025, ali-vilab) | Training-free timestep-embedding-aware feature caching for DiT | ~2x speedup, minimal quality loss; **compatible with LoRAs/ControlNets** | Open source (ref 13) |

**Recommendations:**
- After the multi-view fine-tune converges, run the **Self-Forcing/DMD2 recipe on our own checkpoint**: ≈128 H100-hours ≈ **$300-500** at 2026 prices. Expected multi-view generation latency: tens of seconds per camera batch on one H100/4090-class GPU instead of minutes [estimate — few-step distillation of *camera-conditioned multi-view* models is not yet published; cross-view consistency at 3 steps must be measured. Diffusion stage stops being the bottleneck either way].
- Ship **TeaCache in the ComfyUI nodes from day one** (zero training, works with the undistilled model) as the guaranteed ~2x.
- Adopt **VSA** (FastVideo, Apache 2.0) during fine-tuning if attention cost dominates — it is trainable sparsity, so it belongs in the training plan, not just inference.

## 9.3 Faster Per-Clip 4DGS Optimization (the Second Bottleneck)

| Method | Verified numbers | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid 3D-4D GS** (arXiv 2505.13215) | 10-s clip: **12 min vs 5.5 h** baseline 4DGS; 40-s clip in 52 min; 32.25 dB PSNR, 208 FPS, 273 MB (N3V) | Direct drop-in philosophy for our per-clip stage: classify static Gaussians as 3D, keep only dynamics in 4D — also shrinks input to the 6.6 compression stage (ref 14) |
| **Instant4D** (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2510.01119) | Uncalibrated monocular video → 4DGS in **~5 min incl. preprocessing (≈30x speedup)**; grid pruning −92% Gaussians; 500-900 FPS render | Front-end (MegaSaM + UniDepth) doubles as our missing **moving-camera pose module** (gap #6 in 6.8) (ref 15) |
| **Taming 3DGS** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) | Budgeted, constructive densification: **>5x reduction in training time and model size** | Deterministic Gaussian budget = predictable VRAM for the ComfyUI target (ref 16) |
| **3DGS-MCMC** (NeurIPS 2024) | MCMC relocation replaces heuristic densify/prune | Already available as a **strategy in gsplat** — config change, not new code (ref 17) |
| **gsplat** (arXiv 2409.06765) | Up to **4x less training memory, ~15% less training time** vs reference 3DGS (Mip-NeRF 360); Apache 2.0 | Confirms 6.0 rasterizer choice; memory headroom enables larger per-clip batches (ref 18) |
| **AnySplat** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) | Feed-forward 3DGS from unconstrained views **in seconds**; beats 3DGS *with VGGT initialization* | Use as **initialization** of per-clip optimization → fewer iterations [estimate: init quality on humans unmeasured] (ref 19) |

**Recommendations:** target architecture for the fitting stage = gsplat + MCMC strategy + hard Gaussian budget (Taming-style) + static/dynamic split (Hybrid 3D-4D) + foundation-model initialization. Combined, **per-clip fitting of ~10-min order on one GPU** is supported by published numbers; ~$0.50-1 compute per avatar clip at H100 spot rates [estimate].

**License caveat (verified):** **VGGT-1B original checkpoint is non-commercial; the separate VGGT-1B-Commercial checkpoint permits commercial use (excl. military) under a custom Meta license with an application form** — custom license ≠ Apache/MIT/BSD, so route through the 6.7 attorney review or rely on Instant4D-style SLAM front-ends (MegaSaM/UniDepth licenses: unverified, see Open Questions). MASt3R/DUSt3R are CC BY-NC — **excluded** [B, widely documented; re-verify if needed].

## 9.8 Open Questions

1. Does few-step distillation preserve **cross-view consistency** of a camera-conditioned model? No published result found for multi-view few-step students — measure on our checkpoint (cheap: $500-class experiment, same as 6.8 red-team item 4).
2. Licenses of **MegaSaM / UniDepth** (Instant4D front-end) and ReCamMaster's **MultiCamVideo dataset** — verify before integration (we can re-render our own UE5 data regardless).
3. Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B fine-tune VRAM/cost at our resolutions — single-GPU fine-tuning is documented (ref 8) but multi-view conditioning cost on 5B is unmeasured.
4. FastWan's VSA at 1.3B scale during *fine-tuning* (not distillation): does sparsity interact with multi-view attention patterns?
5. SF Compute live pricing — quote when scheduling the full fine-tune.
