## 22.2 Initialization pipeline — corrected mechanism

The dataset name in the paper is **Dyn4Cam** (self-captured; see §22.7). Initialization is **not** "COLMAP+MASt3R fusion" as an earlier informal characterization implied. The README states verbatim:

> "Since COLMAP produces extremely sparse point clouds with few input views, we use MASt3R-based reconstruction instead."

✅ Confirmed directly against the README. MASt3R **replaces** COLMAP's (insufficient) sparse point cloud — COLMAP's file format is still used to store camera poses, but the actual geometry initialization comes from MASt3R. Notably, the repo does not vendor `naver/mast3r` directly; it instructs cloning `anttwo/MAtCha`, a third-party wrapper that internally bundles MASt3R-SfM as one of its components. This detail matters for §22.6 below — it's an extra hop in the dependency chain, not a red herring.

## 22.3 Genlock / synchronization — retraction, could not verify

⚠️ **Retraction**: the vault's 2026-05-31 note characterized 4C4D's sync approach as "software frame re-alignment" (paraphrased from "inherent temporal misalignment → multi-view frame re-alignment"). This pass re-checked specifically for that claim and **found nothing** — the GitHub README has zero mention of genlock, synchronization, or frame alignment, and no WebSearch query (several phrasings tried) surfaced a 4C4D-specific description of the sync mechanism. It is plausible in principle (consumer GoPro-style cameras generally lack hardware genlock), but **there is no confirmed statement of the actual mechanism** in any source reachable this pass. Do not rely on the "software re-alignment" characterization until someone reads the paper's method section directly (arXiv/CVF access is proxy-blocked in this environment).

This is directly relevant to §6.21: our own 2×GoPro plan documents a specific, verified sync recipe (GoPro Labs QR precision-time + audio clap + software cross-correlation, §21.1). 4C4D's paper may describe something comparable, but as of this pass **we cannot confirm what they actually did** — so it should not be cited as a validated precedent for any particular sync technique.

## 22.4 Neural Decaying Function + visibility detection

✅ **Confirmed, high confidence** — directly quoted from the README:

> "We introduce a Neural Decaying Function $f_\theta$, implemented as a lightweight neural network, to adaptively control the opacity decay of Gaussians... This design mitigates the inherent imbalance between geometry and appearance modeling in 4DGS by encouraging the 4DGS gradients to focus more on geometric learning."

A secondary (search-snippet) source adds the visibility-detection layer: a visibility-detection strategy operates in both spatial and temporal domains to identify which 4D Gaussians are visible at the current view/timestep, applying the Neural Decaying Function only to visible regions while using a separate decay strategy for invisible Gaussians. This second detail is ~confirmed (secondary), not directly read.

**What it actually is, in plain terms**: a small learned network that gates how fast a Gaussian's opacity is allowed to decay, conditioned on visibility, so that gradient signal is steered toward getting the *geometry* right rather than letting appearance-fitting dominate — a targeted fix for a known 4DGS failure mode (geometry/appearance imbalance under sparse-view supervision).

## 22.5 Benchmarks

Structure ~confirmed (secondary): the paper evaluates on **Neural3DV (N3V)** and **ENeRF-Outdoor**, reports PSNR/DSSIM/LPIPS, and compares against baselines named **4DGaussians, Ex4DGS, ST-GS, and 4DGS**, claiming superior performance over all four.

⚠️ **Specific numbers unverified**: an earlier informal note cited "PSNR 22.29 (N3V) / 24.32 (ENeRF-Outdoor)." Targeted searches for those exact figures in this pass returned nothing that confirms or contradicts them — no source quoted the actual results table. **Do not cite these numbers as fact.** If exact benchmark numbers are needed for a pitch or comparison table, they must be pulled from the actual paper PDF by someone with unblocked network access.

Cross-checked against the original Inria `LICENSE.md`: use is granted only "for research purposes," "non-exclusive," "without right to sublicense" — i.e. non-commercial. | `.../diff-gaussian-rasterization/setup.py`, `.../cuda_rasterizer/rasterizer_impl.cu`, `graphdeco-inria/diff-gaussian-rasterization/LICENSE.md` |

**Net effect**: the repo-root MIT license cannot and does not relicense the vendored third-party code — it reads as simply copied from the upstream project and never customized for 4C4D's actual dependency set. `diff-gaussian-rasterization` and `simple-knn` retain Inria's explicit "non-commercial, research/evaluation only" terms in the source itself and are mandatory for training/rendering. MASt3R (bundled inside MAtCha) is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and is needed to initialize any *new* custom footage (the one pre-processed N3V dataset sidesteps this only because it ships precomputed). **Research/testing 4C4D is fine. Shipping any part of this pipeline — or a derivative — in our commercial Track A is blocked by at minimum two, arguably three, non-commercial dependencies, despite the top-level MIT file.**

**Why this connects directly to §6.7**: our own legal-strategy doc's Component License Map (§6.7 "Architectural Recommendations for MVP") exists precisely to audit *every* dependency, not just the top-level LICENSE badge — the same discipline that caught Anny's SMPL-X-topology mode being non-commercial-only despite Anny's own Apache-2.0 top license (§7.1 of 6.7), and that keeps `diff-gaussian-rasterization`/`simple-knn` off our commercial fitting stack in favor of gsplat (§6.7 §7.4, §6.16 §16.2). 4C4D is a textbook confirmation that "check the root LICENSE file" is not sufficient due diligence — it is the *same trap shape* as Anny, just in a different repo. It also underscores why our own project's NC-track use of MASt3R/VGGT for turntable pose-recovery (§6.21 §21.6, defect #5) must stay firewalled behind the NC/commercial boundary (§6.12 firewall, §6.7 Clean-Room plan) and never leak into Track A tooling by convenience — 4C4D shows exactly what that leak looks like when nobody audits past the root license.

## 22.15 Relevance to our project

**On 4C4D's Neural Decaying Function — worth evaluating, but as a technique, not as code.** The confirmed mechanism (§22.4) — a small learned network gating opacity decay based on visibility, to bias gradients toward geometry over appearance under sparse-view supervision — targets a real, named problem class (geometry/appearance imbalance in sparse-view 4DGS) that our own 2×GoPro plan will hit directly: §6.21's "main quality risk" is exactly flicker/floaters from inconsistent generated back-views (§21.6 defect #3), and §6.21 already commits to temporal regularizers (§6.3) as the mitigation without a specific opacity-side mechanism. A Neural-Decaying-Function-style module is a plausible, small, clean-room-reimplementable addition to that regularizer stack — it sits at the loss/opacity-handling layer, which §6.7's Clean-Room plan already scopes as "Module 4: Composite Loss Function" (2-3 weeks, spec-from-paper, no reference code). **Recommendation: add "visibility-gated opacity decay net" as a candidate sub-task under §6.3/§6.19's temporal-regularizer work, specified from the paper's stated mechanism only — never from 4C4D's actual (NC-encumbered) source.** Do not import or adapt any 4C4D code directly, per §22.6.

**On 4C4D's license trap — the more important takeaway is procedural, not technical.** It is a live, concrete confirmation that our §6.7 dependency-level audit discipline (not just root-LICENSE checking) is necessary, not paranoid — the exact same shape of trap (permissive top license, non-commercial vendored core) already caught us once with Anny's SMPL-X mode (§7.1). Any time a new "MIT/Apache" research repo is considered for Track A, the audit must walk `environment.yml`/`requirements`/vendored subfolders, not stop at the root LICENSE file. Worth codifying as an explicit checklist item in §6.7 if it isn't already framed that generally.

**On 4DV.ai — a reference point and a cautionary tale, not a direct competitive threat to our current MVP scope.** 4DV.ai targets a different segment: large-format (20-60+ camera), studio-controlled, cloud-processed volumetric capture for enterprise/XR clients — not a feed-forward, single-workstation, 2-camera consumer pipeline with generative back-hallucination, which is our actual product shape (§6.16, §6.21). Its relevance to us is threefold, all indirect: (1) it validates that well-funded, technically serious players are converging on FreeTimeGS-class temporal-persistence Gaussians, which is useful signal that the underlying research direction (not the specific closed product) is commercially credible; (2) its format/ToS opacity is a pattern to actively differentiate against if we ever position our tooling as more open (Apache/MIT stack, documented license map) to studios weighing both; (3) it is a live example — now sharper per the NAB 2026 "ready to ship" messaging (§22.13) — of the exact reputational risk §6.11 already flags for Gracia-adjacent conversations: overselling capture-hardware cost/simplicity while commercial terms stay undisclosed. **No change to our own roadmap is indicated by 4DV.ai specifically** — it is an out-of-segment reference, useful for partner/investor conversations, not a reason to alter the 2×GoPro MVP plan.
